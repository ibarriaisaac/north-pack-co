Whether you're crossing streams, slogging through mud, or navigating a rainy ridgeline, the right waterproof hiking boots keep you moving comfortably and safely. Best Overall: Salomon X Ultra 4 GTX

The Salomon X Ultra 4 GTX Salomon X Ultra 4 GTX has been a gold standard in trail footwear for years, and the fourth generation refines what was already an exceptional boot. It uses Gore-Tex Extended Comfort waterproofing — one of the most breathable Gore-Tex membranes available — which makes a real difference on multi-hour hikes where heat and sweat build up inside cheaper waterproof liners.

The outsole uses Salomon's Contagrip MA rubber compound, which grips reliably on wet rock, loose gravel, and packed dirt. The Advanced Chassis underfoot adds torsional stability without making the boot feel stiff or clunky. Most hikers find the fit snug through the heel and midfoot with enough toe room to prevent black toenails on descents.

Who it's for: Day hikers and weekend backpackers who want a dependable, versatile boot that handles most North American trail conditions without specialization in any single direction. Pros: Excellent grip, breathable waterproofing, stable platform, wide color and size range Cons: Narrower fit won't work for everyone, pricier than entry-level options

The Scarpa Zodiac Plus GTX Scarpa Zodiac Plus GTX bridges the gap between hiking boot and mountaineering boot with a stiff midsole, precise fit, and a construction that handles serious terrain without becoming unwearable on approach trails.

The rand wraps around the full perimeter of the boot, protecting both the upper and your foot against rock edges. How to Choose the Right Waterproof Hiking Boot

Waterproofing membrane: Gore-Tex is the industry benchmark for breathability and durability. Proprietary membranes cost less and perform adequately in moderate conditions but may lag in sustained exposure. Boot height: Low-cut boots are lighter and better for easy terrain. Mid-cut offers ankle support for most hiking. High-cut suits heavy loads and rough ground. Fit: Try boots on with your hiking socks in the afternoon when your feet are slightly swollen. You should have roughly a thumb's width of space ahead of your longest toe. Outsole: Vibram rubber is the most trusted name for traction. Lug depth and pattern matter — deeper lugs for mud and loose terrain, shallower patterns for rock. Weight vs. support: Lighter boots reduce fatigue on long days but sacrifice structure. Heavier, stiffer boots protect your feet on technical terrain and heavy loads.
