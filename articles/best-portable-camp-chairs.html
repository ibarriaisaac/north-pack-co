Best Portable Camp Chairs

Portable camp chairs are one of those pieces of gear you don't appreciate until you've spent an evening sitting on a log. The right chair makes a real difference whether you're base camping, tailgating, attending a festival, or setting up at a remote backcountry site. This guide covers the best options across different use cases, weight classes, and budgets — so you can stop guessing and start sitting comfortably.

Best Ultralight Backpacking Chair: Helinox Chair One

The Helinox Chair One Helinox Chair One has become the gold standard for backpackers who refuse to sacrifice their seat at the end of a long trail day. It weighs just under two pounds and packs down to roughly the size of a water bottle, making it a genuinely viable addition to a backpacking kit without blowing your weight budget.

The frame is a DAC aluminum alloy pole system — the same company that makes poles for premium tents — so it handles weight far beyond what you'd expect from something this light. The seat fabric is breathable and supportive enough for extended sitting. Setup takes about a minute once you've done it a few times.

The trade-off is price. This is not a budget buy. It's also not ideal for people with limited mobility since the low seat height requires some flexibility to get in and out of comfortably. If weight is your top priority and you're willing to invest, nothing in this category beats it.

Weight: 1 lb 14 oz | Packed Size: 14 x 4 x 4 in | Capacity: 320 lbs

Best for Minimalist Comfort: REI Co-op Flexlite Air

The REI Co-op Flexlite Air REI Co-op Flexlite Air splits the difference between extreme minimalism and genuine comfort better than most chairs in its class. It uses a mesh seat that conforms to your body and breathes well in warm weather — something the Helinox's solid fabric doesn't do quite as effectively.

At just over a pound, it's actually lighter than the Chair One. The frame uses a similar aluminum hub system and folds into a compact carry bag. The seat angle feels slightly more reclined, which some users prefer for relaxed camp evenings but others find less ideal for eating or working at a camp table.

If you're an REI member, the pricing becomes especially competitive. It's a strong all-around pick for three-season backpacking and lightweight camping.

What to Look For Before You Buy

The best camp chair for you depends almost entirely on how you get to your campsite. If you're hiking in, weight and packed size should drive your decision — look at the Helinox or Flexlite Air first. If you're driving to a designated site, prioritize seat comfort, height, and durability over grams saved. For families, features like armrests, drink holders, and high weight capacities matter more than packability. Set a firm budget before you start shopping — there are solid options at every price point, and it's easy to overspend chasing marginal feature upgrades you won't actually use.
