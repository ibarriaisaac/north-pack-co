Best Multi-Tools for Camping

Whether you're setting up a basecamp for a week or heading out for a quick overnight, a quality multi-tool can replace a small pile of individual gear. The right one handles everything from fixing a broken tent pole to preparing food to making trailside repairs on the fly. This guide cuts through the noise and focuses on the tools that actually hold up in the field.

Leatherman Wave Plus — Best Overall

The Leatherman Wave Plus Leatherman Wave Plus remains the benchmark for camping multi-tools, and it earns that status by being genuinely excellent rather than just popular. Its 18 tools include two knife blades — a straight edge and a serrated edge — that both deploy from the outside without opening the handles. That single feature matters more than most people realize until they've fumbled with a standard multi-tool in the dark or with cold hands.

The pliers are full-size and strong enough for serious work, and the wire cutters are replaceable, which extends the tool's usable life well beyond what you'd get from competitors. At around 8.5 ounces, it sits in the middle of the weight range — not an ultralight option, but not punishing either.

The Wave Plus also comes with a nylon or leather sheath depending on the version you choose, and it's compatible with Leatherman's bit kit and extender accessories if you want to expand functionality later. Built in the USA and backed by a 25-year warranty, this is the tool you buy once and forget about replacing.

Best for: Campers who want one tool that handles everything without compromise.

---

Victorinox SwissChamp — Best for Knife-Centric Campers

The Victorinox SwissChamp Victorinox SwissChamp takes a fundamentally different approach than most multi-tools. Rather than building around pliers, it organizes around blades and precision tools, making it the stronger choice for campers who need fine cutting tasks done well — food prep, cordage work, carved tent stakes — more than mechanical repairs.

With 33 functions packed into its layered Swiss construction, the SwissChamp includes both a large and small blade, a wood saw that genuinely cuts through branches, scissors, a fish scaler, magnifying glass, and even a small chisel. The scissors, in particular, are far better than anything you'll find on plier-based multi-tools — they open and close cleanly and hold an edge well.

The tradeoff is that there are no locking blades on most Swiss Army models, including this one. For light camp tasks that's manageable, but anyone who prioritizes safety during heavy cutting should factor that in. It also doesn't include full-size pliers, which is a real gap for certain repairs.

At around 6 ounces, it's lighter than most full-featured alternatives, and its slim profile stores easily in a pack's hip belt pocket or chest strap attachment.

Best for: Campers and backpackers who prioritize blade variety and food prep over mechanical repair capability.

---

Gerber Suspension NXT — Best Budget Pick

If you're new to camping or simply don't want to spend north of $80 on a multi-tool, the Gerber Suspension NXT Gerber Suspension NXT delivers a solid set of 15 tools at a price point that's easy to justify. It includes spring-loaded pliers, a partially serrated knife, scissors, a saw, and a set of screwdrivers that handle most camp chores without issue.

The butterfly-open design feels intuitive, and the aluminum handles are comfortable for extended use. For the price, the blade quality is acceptable — not impressive, but it holds an edge through a weekend trip and resharpens easily with a basic stone.

Where it falls short is longevity and refinement. The tool actions aren't as smooth as Leatherman's, and the wire cutters aren't replaceable. But for a first multi-tool or a backup kept in the car or camp kitchen kit, the Suspension NXT earns its place without apology.

Best for: Budget-minded campers, beginners, or anyone who wants a capable backup tool.

---

Leatherman Surge — Best for Heavy-Duty Tasks

When the work gets serious — repairing camp stoves, cutting heavy cable, working with thicker cordage or hardware — the Leatherman Surge Leatherman Surge is the tool you want in your hand. It's Leatherman's largest multi-tool, and it's built with that reality fully embraced rather than apologized for.

The Surge's blade is longer and thicker than the Wave Plus, and its pliers handle more torque. It includes large bit drivers compatible with the full Leatherman bit kit, which means it can sub in for a proper driver set at a base camp or car camping setup. The outside-accessible blades carry over from the Wave Plus design, maintaining that key usability advantage.

At just over 12 ounces, this isn't a backpacking tool. But for car campers, overlanders, and anyone running a more permanent setup where weight isn't the primary concern, its raw capability is hard to match in a single handheld package.

Best for: Car campers, overlanders, and anyone tackling demanding mechanical tasks in the field.

---

SOG PowerAccess Deluxe — Best for Gear-Heavy Backpackers

The SOG PowerAccess Deluxe SOG PowerAccess Deluxe targets the backpacker who carries technical gear — trekking poles with adjustment screws, stoves with hardware fittings, bikes on the trail — and needs a wider range of bit-driver options than most multi-tools provide. Its built-in bit driver accepts standard ¼-inch hex bits, giving it unusual versatility for precision adjustments.

SOG's compound leverage pliers require less hand strength to use than conventional designs, which makes a difference during long trips when fatigue accumulates. The tool itself is well-balanced and includes 21 tools total, covering the standard bases alongside its standout driver system.

The knife blade is serviceable rather than exceptional, and the scissors feel slightly less refined than Victorinox's. But as a repair and adjustment tool built around real backpacking scenarios, the PowerAccess Deluxe fills a specific gap that the more general-purpose options don't address as neatly.

Best for: Technical backpackers who need precise hardware adjustments and broad driver compatibility.

---

Leatherman Skeletool CX — Best Ultralight Option

Ounces add up fast on a long-distance trail, and the Leatherman Skeletool CX Leatherman Skeletool CX is built for campers who track every one of them. At just 5 ounces, it delivers the core tools most people actually use — pliers, a combination knife blade, a carabiner clip, a bit driver, and a bottle opener — without the extra weight of functions that rarely leave the handle.

The carbon fiber and stainless construction keeps weight low without feeling flimsy, and the carabiner body doubles as a clip-point for attaching it directly to a pack strap or gear loop. The single combo blade locks securely and deploys from outside the handle.

What you're giving up is breadth. No scissors, no saw, no file, no can opener. If your camp tasks stay close to the basics, none of that matters. If you tend to find uses for the full toolkit, the Skeletool will eventually leave you wishing for more.

Best for: Ultralight backpackers and minimalist campers who prioritize weight savings without dropping multi-tool capability entirely.

---

How to Choose the Right Multi-Tool for Your Camp Style

Before you buy, narrow your decision by answering three questions: How much does your pack weight matter? What tasks are you most likely to perform? And how much are you willing to spend?

Weight-conscious backpackers should look at the Skeletool CX first and move up only if the missing tools are genuinely necessary for their trips. Car campers and base campers can afford the Surge or Wave Plus without penalty. Knife-first users who mostly prep food and cut cordage will often be happier with the SwissChamp than a plier-centric tool that doesn't cut as well. And if budget is the binding constraint, the Gerber Suspension NXT handles real work at a price that leaves room for other gear.

Any of these tools will outlast a season of careless reviews and vague rankings — pick the one that fits how you actually camp, not how you imagine you might.
